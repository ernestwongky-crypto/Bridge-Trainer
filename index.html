Run
One leg, 12 kn, on track. One contact at a time; the drill skips ahead between contacts and ends at the wheel-over

Your margins: CPA 2.5 cables, bow crossing 8 cables. One contact at a time: lookout calls up and waits for "OOW", nav follows straight on, then it is your call. Nothing else is reported until the ship is steady on your ordered course (helm: "On course …"), you order a speed, or you give a sitrep ("Watch-on-deck, sitrep … Watch-on-deck") whose intention is to maintain course and speed. "Carry on" or tapping the status bar also moves on. Say "check port / starboard / stern" for the lookout, "check two five nine" for nav's blind check, "nav" for a follow-up, "lookout" for bearing movement, "nav bow crossing range", "check starboard, check zero one zero", then declare it clear and give the order, e.g. "starboard clear, two five nine clear, starboard fifteen, slow ahead both". Engine orders: "step eight ahead both engines" (step 10 = 12 kn, step 1 slowest), "stop both engines". "Very good" is an acknowledgement. Nav answers "nav, report CPA", "nav, on track?" (by PI line) and "nav, range to wheelover"; nav never gives the course to regain track. Bow crossing under 8 cables: "CO sir, request to pass five cables ahead of her".

Keys stay only in this browser and go only to Deepgram and Anthropic. Set orders are handled instantly; questions and free phrasing go to Claude. Earphones give the cleanest call.

Voice orders: with Voice call on, just talk. Without it, press Talk (or the space bar on a laptop); Hands-free keeps the mic open and pauses it while the team speaks.

Contacts are different every run: crossing from either side, head-on or overtaking, some passing clear and some a real risk. Geography is approximate, traced from a public MPA chart, and not for navigation.
